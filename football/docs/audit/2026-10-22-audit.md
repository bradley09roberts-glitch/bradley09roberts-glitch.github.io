# Football predictor: audit report

This audit was read-only and was carried out on 22 Oct 2026. It covers `/home/user/bradley09roberts-glitch.github.io/football` on branch `claude/football-predictor` (HEAD `fda1eff`). Times are in UTC unless marked UK. UK clocks go back on Sunday 25 Oct.

**Severity scale.**
- **Critical:** wrong predictions or records published, leakage, data corruption, or secrets or prices exposed.
- **High:** materially wrong numbers or claims shown to the owner, or broken scheduled operations.
- **Medium:** a real bug with limited impact, or misleading UI.
- **Low:** minor.

Every severity below is the final, corrected one: the skeptic's where a skeptic reviewed the finding, otherwise the verifier's. Items found by more than one area are merged. The original finding IDs follow each item in [brackets] so the developer can trace the scratch evidence under `scratchpad/audit2/`.

---

## 1. Summary

The forecasting core is sound. We found no leakage of future results, and all 1,089 published predictions were made before kick-off. Every one of them replays bit for bit from its code and data, and the headline accuracy and benchmark numbers recompute exactly. The real problems are in day-to-day running and in how results are described.

**Operations.**
- All scheduled check-ins are one-off reminders, so publishing and settling stop after Tuesday 27 Oct. The 31 Oct to 2 Nov weekend and Champions League matchday 4 will get no records unless that is fixed.
- From Monday the Past page will list each match up to 14 times, once per model, with a pooled and misleading tally.
- Around 2 to 5 Nov your phone copy will hit its 64 MB size limit and stop updating.

**Data handling.** Several weak spots would damage the record if triggered:
- A rebuilt database would republish matches under reused ids.
- One odd Wikipedia score can delete stored UEFA matches.
- La Liga's "everything on Sunday 25 Oct" dates are placeholders. They will cost Friday's market records their prices, and will later double-count games.

**For betting.** The league percentages you see are the bookmakers' own consensus with the margin removed. The 53.4% hit rate is exactly what backing the favourite gives, and no model here beats the closing price. The Champions League model has no price input at all, and it under-predicts goals.

**Owner-facing documents.** Several statements are wrong:
- The Wikipedia block is HTTP 403, not a 429 rate limit.
- Odds capture has stored prices.
- UEFA.com still feeds one input.
- The repository holding the records is public, and no decision acknowledges that.

Most fixes are small. The biggest wins are recurring check-ins, an always-on host with off-site backups, and a source of UEFA prices.

#### H3. The phone copy will stop updating around 2 to 5 November
*In plain English:* the static export includes a full page for every prediction ever made. At the shadow-record rate it passes its own 64 MB refusal limit within about two weeks.
- **Where:** src/footy/snapshot.py:74-79 (`HOST_LIMITS`, 64 MB total and 255 files), :413-422 and :635-675 (every prediction gets a page, with no time window), and :963-983 and :1215-1232 (`SnapshotRefused`).
- **Evidence:**
  - The site was 41.25 MB on 22 Oct. A record costs about 5.8 KB unsettled and about 7 KB once settled, and past cards add about 2.9 KB each to `snapshot.json`.
  - From Friday each league fixture carries about 20 records, and each UEFA fixture 6.
  - Projection:

    | Date | Event | Size |
    |---|---|---|
    | Fri 23 Oct | publish | ~44 MB |
    | Mon 26 Oct | settle | ~52 MB |
    | Tue 27 Oct | publish | ~53 MB |
    | Fri 30 Oct | publish | ~60 MB |
    | Mon 2 Nov | settle | ~65.7 MB, refused |

  - It fails safe: the old site is kept and records are unaffected. But the owner's only view freezes, and every check-in ends with this step.

- **Fix:**
  - Keep full pages only for a rolling window (for example the last 30 days plus upcoming fixtures), with record-of-record pages only before that.

  - Add a size projection to the export report.
- **Effort:** M. [api-snapshot-1, ops-7, docs-claims-5, product-accuracy-0]

#### H5. A single unusual Wikipedia score can delete stored UEFA matches, and the job still reports success
*In plain English:* when a 2026/27 Wikipedia match box shows a walkover, awarded, abandoned or postponed result, the loader drops that row. The next rebuild then deletes the stored match, even when it is a played result or a fixture with published records.
- **Where:**
  - src/footy/europe/wikipedia_2627.py:567-574 (`unparsed_score` and `awarded_result` cause a `continue`) and :703.
  - src/footy/europe/build_2627.py:103 (writes the CSV whatever the build errors).
  - src/footy/europe/euro_load.py:655-659 (`has_errors` counts only CSV mapping errors, so `allow_delete=True`) and :411-412.
  - docs/europe/uefa-matches.md:239 claims the loader never deletes after a parse error.
- **Evidence:**
  - Scratch copy, with `read_raw` patched:
    - Bournemouth v Milan (id 4017, 6 published records) set to "Abandoned" or "3–0 (awd.)" gives `deleted 1, partial False`. `settle()` then settles 0, and the 6 records stay pending forever.
    - A played match (Ararat-Armenia 1-4 Sparta) is deleted the same way.
    - Changing "Times are" to "Kick-offs are" on one UECLQ page deleted 150 played rows (258 down to 108), again with `partial False`.
  - Walkovers are never stored as `awarded`.
  - An abandoned match followed by its replay labels the replay "leg 2" and drops the real second leg.
  - This has not happened yet, but it sits on the scheduled `euro-update` path.
- **Fix:**
  - Count every build-stage issue as a load error, so `allow_delete=False` and `ctx.partial=True`.
  - Store walkovers and awarded results as `awarded`, and abandoned or postponed matches as `postponed`.
  - Never delete a played row without writing a `data_corrections` entry.
  - Add a test in which an unparsable box must not delete its stored row.
- **Effort:** S. [gap-uefa-tie-semantics-and-team-stats-0]

## 3. Things I don't like

Design, process and product choices worth changing, with the reason and an alternative for each.

**D1. Live operations run on one-off reminders fired into one busy development session (medium).**
- **Reason:**
  - That session holds about 455k tokens and the container restarts every hour or two (commits da289d7 and ba55a08 are restart checkpoints).
  - Publishing the shadow records takes 27 minutes, and nothing alerts the owner when a check-in fails.
  - No scheduled operation has failed yet: each fired and was acted on within about a minute.
  - D-009's "not needed until a provider key exists" no longer holds, because M7 now depends on scheduled jobs.
  - Firing each check-in into a fresh session doesn't help, because a fresh session has no data/.
- **Alternative:**
  - An always-on host: a VPS at about €5–6 a month, or the owner's always-on PC or Pi.
  - systemd timers in Europe/London time running the idempotent CLI jobs, with a healthchecks.io ping per job.
  - Nightly off-site backups and a `footy doctor` report.
  - Claude keeps a weekly review and the artifact publish.
  - In the meantime: recurring tasks plus a Friday safety re-run at about 16:30.
- [ops-5, product-accuracy-1]

**D2. Operational code and data sit outside version control (medium).**
- **Reason:**
  - capture_loop.sh is git-ignored, and it runs `uv run` without `--frozen`.
  - The Overnight report builder exists only in the scratchpad and hard-codes its model order.
  - The openfootball clones appear in no setup step.
  - STATUS's publishing steps hard-code this session's scratchpad path.
  - Killed jobs stay "running" for ever.
  - On a new machine all of these fail, and the steps to restore them are written nowhere.
- **Alternative:**
  - Put the scripts in `scripts/` and `tools/` in the repo, and add `footy bootstrap`.
  - Use relative paths.
  - Mark stale "running" jobs as killed.
- [ops-10, data-12]

**D3. Fixed rules from the brief were changed without a decision entry (medium).**
- **Reason:**
  - "Odds independence: bookmaker prices never enter the primary model" no longer holds. Both models of record use prices (league weights 1.0/1.0, and UEFA xG fitted to closing prices), and no entry cites or amends the rule. The basis is the owner's "best accuracy" request (STATUS:7-8). D-024 keeps the pure model's own record, so the comparison survives.
  - "Early edition" was redefined, and a rolling 3-year training window (walkforward.py:56) replaced "expands through 2022/23". The composite headline also sits awkwardly with "never pool lines into one figure"; D-025 records the composite but not the conflict.
  - Price shadows were "the owner's call" in model-comparison.md:51-53, but STATUS #12 says "decided 22 Oct". DECISIONS has no shadow-records entry at all, and D-028 "proposed" is missing from the Open table. The owner did say "Do what's best" and asked to see every model.
- **Alternative:**
  - A D-029 plus a "Deviations from the brief" table, giving each change with its reason and owner status.
  - Name an odds-free "pure model of record" with its own market benchmark and profit run.
- [gap-owner-artifacts-1, gap-owner-artifacts-5, docs-claims-10, gap-owner-artifacts-4]

**D4. A bet was picked in chat (medium).**
- **Reason:**
  - At 19:00 on 21 Oct the reply opened with "I wouldn't put money on any of these as a value bet … no proven edge". It then gave "fair odds" from the price-blind UEFA model and a rule that "longer than the fair price = value".
  - It picked Inter as "the one where we disagree most with ChatGPT" and added "now or never for tonight". The owner bet. Inter won 1-0.
  - Backing the largest disagreement with a market-informed forecast is adverse selection.
  - The six picks' model probabilities sum to 4.46 expected wins, so "five of six landed" is unremarkable.
  - Neither the suggestions nor the prices are recorded anywhere.
- **Alternative:**
  - A standing rule in STATUS or CLAUDE.md: no picks and no value thresholds in chat; cite the market benchmark; offer the de-vigged consensus if asked; never add urgency; mention deposit limits and GamStop.
  - If the owner wants tips tracked, record each one as an immutable pre-match selection with its price and time, and withhold returns until there are 20 or more.
- [docs-claims-6, security-compliance-3]

**D5. ml_online_market is a hard switch presented as a learner (medium).**
- **Reason:**
  - At the live cutoff its weights are one-hot: the GLM booster gets 1.0 in F1 and I1, the shots model 0.99999978 in SP1, and ctx 0.983 in E0. Its La Liga records are therefore essentially market_tilt_shots.
  - Records store only eta and share, not the weights.
  - The owner-facing note "weights relearn after every result" oversells it. The research doc already admits it.
- **Alternative:**
  - Store the learned weights and the stackers' coefficients in each record, and flag a mixture as degenerate when one member holds more than 99%.
  - Re-tuning with a weight floor is a new candidate and must go through the protocol.
- [ml-shadows-4]

**D6. The Fixtures page doesn't answer "who should I back this weekend?" (medium).**
- **Reason:**
  - At 390 px the page is 114 cards and about 70 screens tall, and it opens with yesterday's games that have already kicked off. The first Premier League card is 28.7 screens down.
  - Boilerplate repeats on every card: 113 say "No odds available", and all 114 say "Early edition" and "Without lineups".
  - 37 of the 114 PICK tags sit on outcomes under 45%, and 9 within 5 points of the next outcome (Como 36.2 v Man United 38.6 is tagged PICK).
- **Alternative:**
  - A compact "At a glance" list and a "This weekend" view.
  - Collapse kicked-off games into a "Results pending" row.
  - Show "Close call" instead of PICK when the gap is under 5 points.
  - One page-level note instead of per-card boilerplate.
- [frontend-ux-5]

**D7. Jargon and internal IDs reach the owner, and explanations are hover-only (medium).**
- **Reason:**
  - Notes print verbatim with decision IDs (1,044 of 1,089 records).
  - There is a "Moneyline" tab, and "RPS 0.1959, lower is better" has no yardstick.
  - "Verdict · D-028 (proposed)" appears, raw model codes show on Performance and Past, and four near-synonyms are used for the headline model.
  - The run picker lists 134 internal runs.
  - Badge meanings exist only as `title` tooltips, which never show on a phone, and there is no glossary.
- **Alternative:**
  - A glossary and tap popovers, with `model_label` everywhere and one term ("headline model").
  - Rename Moneyline to "Match result" and give RPS a yardstick.
  - Default the picker to the models of record.
  - When notes move into "Record details", keep "does not beat the closing price" visible.
- [frontend-ux-6]

**D8. The frontend has no tests or lint, and D-004's type-safety claim is false (medium).**
- **Reason:**
  - 24.8k lines with no test or lint script.
  - The TypeScript types are hand-written and FastAPI returns untyped dicts, so drift can't be caught.
  - The snapshot's set of requests mirrors the frontend by hand, so a new request in api.ts breaks nothing in CI and shows "Not in this snapshot" on the phone.
  - CI never builds the snapshot variant.
- **Alternative:**
  - vitest for the pure modules, with shared canonical-key fixtures read by both pytest and vitest.
  - A Playwright smoke test over `make snapshot`, and eslint with react-hooks.
  - Generated types from OpenAPI, or correct D-004.
- [code-quality-tests-6]

**D9. The "price in disguise" guard relies on a hand-kept list (low).**
- **Reason:**
  - The export's pairwise consensus check can't catch a single model forgotten from `PRICE_IDENTICAL`; this was reproduced, with 380 cells exposed.
  - `is_market_only` is applied in only some routes.
- **Alternative:** derive price-identity from each run's manifest, compare against a known consensus column, and centralise the market-only filter. [api-snapshot-4]

**D10. ml_stack_market's per-match figures are shown although they sit within about 1 point of the consensus (low).**

- **Alternative:** the owner chooses an explicit threshold, for example withhold a column when its median gap is under 0.02. [api-snapshot-10]

## 7. Method

**Areas, 13 in all.** Each had its own auditor:
- modelling
- ML learners and shadow records
- data pipeline and settlement
- API and static snapshot
- website UX and accessibility
- operations
- documentation and claims
- security, licensing and responsible use
- code quality and tests
- product strategy and accuracy
- replay of every published record
- owner artifacts and brief compliance
- UEFA tie semantics and team statistics

**Review chain.** An independent verifier re-checked every finding. A skeptic tried to refute each high or critical finding (and some medium ones). This report merges duplicates and uses the final severity: the skeptic's where one ran, otherwise the verifier's.

**What was run.** Everything ran on copies of the database (with WAL) under `scratchpad/audit2/`, with scratch `FOOTY_DATA_DIR`.
- **Tests and static checks:** the full pytest suite (893 passed, 3 skipped), coverage (90% of 14,471 statements), ruff, mypy and eslint on a copy.
- **Recomputation:** from the saved ledgers, with paired block bootstraps at half-week and monthly blocks, of composites, RPS, log loss, hit rates and the closing-price benchmark, plus score-grid coherence on all 134 runs.
- **Leakage:** custom scramble tests that also scrambled match statistics, across 29 domestic candidates and the UEFA mixtures.
- **Replay:** of all 1,089 published records, using as-of database reconstructions and each record's own commit (bit-identical once the true code was used).
- **API and snapshot:** uvicorn and TestClient runs against all endpoints (timing, path traversal, error handling), and full `export-snapshot` runs, including on a copy with simulated settlement.
- **Website:** Playwright at 390 and 1280 px in light and dark themes, axe-core 4.10.2, keyboard tours, and throttled load timings.
- **Simulations and reproductions:**
  - settlement simulations (mid-match score, corrections, awarded matches);
  - concurrent-settle races;
  - database-rebuild republishing;
  - capture outage;
  - fd-fixtures throttle;
  - UEFA loader deletion via patched raw pages;
  - synthetic Wikipedia match boxes;
  - fixture price joins.
- **Read-only inspection:** scheduled tasks (`list_triggers` and `get_trigger`, read only), the session's metadata, the GitHub API (repository visibility and push times), `fetch_log`, `job_runs`, capture.log, the openfootball clones, and the published artifacts (read only).

**Constraints kept.**
- Nothing in the repository was edited, created or deleted. No commits, stashes or checkouts were made, and no records were published. `git status` is clean.
- One auditor's `git fetch origin` updated remote-tracking refs under `.git`; the working tree was untouched.
- No Wikimedia site or uefa.com was contacted.
- Secrets in `football/.env` were checked for presence only. The Sportmonks token was matched without being printed.

**Not verified.**
- The exact chat wording behind some claims.
- Upstream openfootball changes after 22 Oct 16:50.
- Whether the Wikipedia 403 comes from Wikimedia or from the egress proxy.
- Provider terms and tiers not recorded in the repo (Betfair BASIC, API-Football).
