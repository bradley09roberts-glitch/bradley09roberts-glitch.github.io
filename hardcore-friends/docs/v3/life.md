# Village life: the calendar, feasts, market day, music, birthdays, funerals and the Village Chronicle (3.0)

The village now has a life of its own and a history. There is a calendar with weeks and seasons; a market day every
week; three feasts a year at the square, with food from the stores, music and a speech; birthdays; music on a note
block in the evenings; and when someone dies for good, a funeral, a grave at the cemetery and a few days of mourning.
Everything that happens is written down in the **Village Chronicle**, a real book kept at the town hall. All of it
happens by itself: you never have to give an order, and you are welcome to join in.

Nothing here has been run in the game yet. See **Honest limits** at the end.

## The calendar

The calendar follows the overworld's own day count (the clock that keeps going through nights you sleep through), so
it needs no setting up. Day 1 is the world's first day.

- **A week** has seven days, Monday to Sunday. **Saturday is market day.**
- **A year** has four seasons, spring, summer, autumn and winter, of 10 days each (40 days a year; `daysPerSeason`
  changes it).
- **Three feasts:** the **midsummer feast** in the middle of summer, the **harvest festival** on the last day of autumn
  (only if the farms did well: enough crops harvested since autumn began, or the stores full), and the **winter
  lights** in the middle of winter.
- **Music** is played every other evening (Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday) and at every feast, but not on the
  evening of a funeral.

`/friends calendar` shows today's date ("Sunday, day 42: the 2nd day of spring, year 2"), what is on today, and the
next ten days: feasts, market days, birthdays and funerals. `/friends camp` has a line about it too.

Before the village has a town plan, everything happens at the camp: the square is the open ground round the camp
centre, and the Chronicle is kept in the supply chest.

## Feasts

On a feast day the news goes round in the morning. In the late afternoon (from about time 9500) everyone free in the
camp, children too, comes to the square and stands in a ring round it. When most are there, someone says a few words:
Sage, or whoever has been with the camp longest. Then it is a party until nightfall:

- **Food.** That afternoon the **cook** (the innkeeper, or the camp's cook, or else the farmer) goes to the supply
  chest, bakes bread from spare wheat at the crafting table, and takes food for everyone (the best first: cooked meat,
  pies, fish, bread). At the feast they hand it round, one portion each, to everyone a little hungry. They only take
  what the camp can spare: never more than half the food in the chest, half as much when food is getting short, and
  none when the camp is hungry (then people eat what they carry). Food not eaten goes back to the chest afterwards.
- **Company and fun** fill up for everyone at the square; there are hearts, cheering, and children running about.
- **Music.** The village's musician plays at every feast (see Music).
- **The winter lights.** On the winter feast one friend puts up to eight lanterns (or torches, only from what the chest
  holds beyond 24, which stay for the camp's own lighting) in a ring round the square at dusk. They glow all night and are taken down the next morning and go back to the
  chest.

At nightfall the speaker says good night, everyone goes home to bed, the feast goes in the Chronicle, and the camp gains
a little Unity (10). A monster near the square holds the party up until it is dealt with; a feast that nobody could
come to is simply missed. The night watch never leaves the watch for a feast.

## Market day

Every Saturday, from morning to the late afternoon, once the village has stalls (a market square, or someone keeping a
shop: the shopkeeper, baker, butcher, fishmonger, tailor or smith from the market part of 3.0):

- **Shopkeepers keep their stalls**: they spend more of the day standing by their shop's counter, calling out to
  passers-by. When you walk up, they hand over to their usual shop-keeping, which opens the trading screen as always.
- **Everyone else looks round the market** once in their spare time: they wander over to a stall (or the square) and
  chat for a while, which lifts their spirits.

Prices and stock stay exactly as the market part keeps them: market day adds no goods and changes no prices.

## Music

In the evening the village's **musician** plays a few tunes on a note block: someone wearing a bard's clothes (one of
the village skins), else the innkeeper, else a redstone tinkerer such as Spark, else the chattiest friend. They take a
note block from the supply chest (or make one at the crafting table from eight planks and a redstone dust), put it down
beside them in the tavern (or at the square, before there is a tavern), play two or three old tunes (Twinkle Twinkle,
Frere Jacques, Ode to Joy, Greensleeves), and pick it back up. The notes are the note block's own sounds, with the
instrument set by the block it stands on, and you hear them from a good way off. Everyone nearby enjoys it.

On a feast day there is no music before the feast: the musician waits and plays at the feast itself, leaving the ring
to play once the feast has begun. On the evening of a funeral there is no music at all. A note block left standing (the
musician called away, lost, or gone out of the loaded world mid-tune) is taken down by the tidying-up by day.

## Birthdays

Everyone has a birthday: the day of the year they were born in the camp, or, for everyone else, the day of the year
they joined it. (The nine and the newcomers who were with you before this update count from the day the update first
saw them.) On the day, once they are up and about in the camp:

- everyone is told ("It's Mabel's birthday in the camp: 1 year since they joined. Happy birthday!");
- up to three friends nearby wish them a happy birthday, and they thank everyone;
- if the camp has plenty of food, there is a present: a **cake** from the supply chest (or the bakery's shelves), shared
  with everyone nearby, or else a cookie, a pie or an apple for their backpack. Real food from the stores, nothing
  made from thin air;
- children born in the camp have their birthday written in the Chronicle.

## Funerals and graves

When someone of the team dies for good:

- **The Chronicle** tells it gently ("Fern Hart was lost in a fight with a zombie, far from home.").
- **Everyone mourns** for a while: two days for the camp, four for a husband or wife, parents and children. Mourning
  slowly wears down their fun and company (never below a third), and now and then they say they miss the one they lost.
- **A grave** is made, usually the same day or the next morning: a grown-up fetches a headstone (a stone wall post, or a
  stone block; cobblestone only when the camp has plenty), a sign (or makes one from planks and a stick) and a flower or
  two from the supply chest, and puts them up at the **cemetery**: a small plot the friends lay out themselves on level
  natural ground towards the edge of the camp, behind the chapel once the village has one, away from the streets and
  from anything you built. Graves stand in rows facing the camp. The sign reads "In memory of", the name, the day and
  "Rest well", and is waxed so it cannot be changed by accident. Each grave's ground is reserved so nothing is ever
  built over it.
- **The funeral** is held the next evening (from time 10000; on a feast day it comes first, from time 9000, and the
  feast follows it): everyone free gathers in a half ring in front of the grave,
  someone says a few words (Sage, or family, or whoever has been with the camp longest), three friends say goodbye one
  after another, and if a parent was lost, the other parent (or the speaker) promises the children will be looked after.
  Then a moment's quiet, and everyone goes about their evening. If the funeral cannot be held (nobody at the camp, a
  monster about, a storm), it moves to the next evening; after three evenings the one lost is remembered quietly in the
  Chronicle instead.
- **Visits.** While they mourn, friends visit the grave once a day, say a few words and leave a flower if a place by the
  headstone is empty; family keep visiting now and then for good (about one day in five). Children visit too.

The people part of 3.0 already looks after what a death means for a family (a widowed partner, children living with the
other parent): village life adds the funeral, the grave and the words.

## The Village Chronicle

The Chronicle is the village's history, one dated line at a time:

> Day 42, spring: Mabel and Oak were married at the town hall, and became the Hart family.

It records arrivals (the nine joining, newcomers asked in, children growing up), births, weddings, deaths, buildings
finished (the camp's own and the village's: houses, civic buildings, shops and workplaces), the camp growing a stage,
the feasts and funerals, children's birthdays, the steps of Sage's plan (the iron age, the first diamonds, enchanting,
the Nether, blaze rods, ender pearls, the stronghold, the End portal), the first time anyone sets foot in the Nether or
the End, every dragon defeated, and pillager raids beaten off (or not). On a world that already had a camp, the
Chronicle begins with a line about the camp as it is, and only what happens from then on is written.

- `/friends chronicle` shows the latest page in chat, for any player; `/friends chronicle <page>` any other page (page 1
  is the oldest).
- **The book.** The Chronicle's keeper (Sage, else a strategist, else whoever has been with the camp longest) writes it
  out in a real written book, now and then by day when there is news. The first copy needs a **book and quill** from the
  supply chest, or a book (or three paper and a leather), an **ink sac** and a **feather** to make one: put them in the
  chest. Only a **blank** book and quill is used: one with anything written in it, or with a name of its own, is yours
  and is never taken, so your notes are safe in the chest. Until the village has a town hall the book is kept in the supply chest; after that it lies on the **town hall's
  lectern** (if the builders left the lectern out, a lectern from the chest is put on its spot; with none in the chest,
  the keeper tries again the next day). You can read it there like any lectern book. If you put a book of your own on
  that lectern, it is left alone and the Chronicle stays in the chest until you take your book off again.
- A book holds 100 pages. When a volume is full, the keeper writes its last lines in, begins the next one in a new book
  and the full one goes to the supply chest (Volume I, Volume II...).
- If you take the book away, it is yours to keep: the keeper writes the volume out again in a new book from the stores.

## Commands

All work at permission level 0 with cheats off, and only show things.

| Command | What it shows |
|---|---|
| `/friends calendar` | today's date and part of the day, what is on today, and the next ten days |
| `/friends chronicle` | the latest page of the Village Chronicle, and where the book is kept |
| `/friends chronicle <page>` | another page (1 is the oldest) |

## Settings (`config/hardcorefriends.json`)

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `villageLife` | `true` | Feasts, market days, music and birthdays. Off: none of these (the calendar, funerals and the Chronicle still run). |
| `graves` | `true` | A grave at the cemetery for each friend lost. Off: no graves (funerals are held at the square instead). |
| `chronicleBook` | `true` | The Chronicle is also written out as a real book. Off: it is kept for `/friends chronicle` only. |
| `daysPerSeason` | `10` | Days in each of the four seasons (3 to 30). Changing it moves the feasts and birthdays in an existing world. |

## For the other packages

- `life.VillageLife.chronicle(server, text)` writes a line in the Chronicle, dated today ("Raiders came to the village,
  and were driven off."): a short sentence in plain words.
- The jobs: `needs.festival` (going to a feast or funeral), `needs.market` (looking round the market) and
  `needs.remember` (visiting a grave) are needs jobs, so children may take part; `life.feast_cook`, `life.lights`,
  `life.music`, `life.market_stall`, `life.grave`, `life.tidy_up` and `life.chronicle` are grown-ups' jobs. A job
  filter keeps the feast's cook from putting the feast's food back in the chest before the feast.
- Every block change goes through the edit guard's existing rules: BUILD for the graves (headstone, sign, flowers), the
  winter lights, the musician's note block and a missing lectern; INVENT (the friends' own block, the same block) for
  showing or clearing the book on the friends' own lectern. No new rule is registered.
- Each grave reserves its ground as a camp site (`life.grave.N`, a 3 by 3 box round the headstone; the prefix is
  `life.VillageLife.GRAVE_SITES`), so the town plan, the streets and the builders keep off it. Terra's landscaping does
  not count these as buildings still to come (it lights and plants round the graves as anywhere) and lays no path to
  each grave.

## Honest limits

- **Nothing here has been run in the game.** It compiles; every part was re-read for crashes, griefing and stuck loops,
  but how it plays (how well people gather, how long a feast lasts, where the cemetery ends up) is untested, and the
  numbers (scores, times, how much food is shared) are first guesses.
- **Feasts are short.** From the late afternoon to nightfall is about three minutes of real time, and players
  sleeping through the evening ends a feast early. Jobs that are urgent (building in a hurry, the night watch, a
  desperate need) carry on and those friends join later or not at all.
- **The cook brings and bakes; they do not cook meat specially.** "Cooking extra" is baking spare wheat into bread and
  bringing the stores' ready food. The market's innkeeper and the camp's cook keep cooking meat as usual.
- **Market day cannot change prices** (the market part has no way to), and it adds no stock. It only means more time at
  the stalls and more visitors.
- **The cemetery is laid out by the friends**, as the town plan has no cemetery of its own. It needs level natural
  ground inside the camp, away from the square, the streets' lines and anything you built; in a crowded or hilly camp
  a grave may not find a place (it is tried again, six times, and the name stays in the Chronicle). A spot nobody can
  walk to (up a ledge, inside a fenced field) also counts as a failed try; after two, another spot is chosen, and a
  plot with no grave in it yet is laid out somewhere else. A grave that failed waits behind the others until the next
  day, so it never holds up the graves after it. The friends keep
  four blocks off the lines where the village's streets run, from the camp centre and 34 blocks out, which is the town
  plan's grid; the lanes' exact positions are assumed, not asked.
- **Signs** are made as oak signs from any planks (the game would match the wood). A camp with no planks to spare and no
  signs gets a plain headstone.
- **Flowers** for graves only come from the supply chest: friends do not pick wild flowers for them.
- **The book needs an ink sac and a feather** (or a book and quill), which the friends do not gather themselves:
  fishing now and then brings up an ink sac, feathers come from chickens. Until then the Chronicle lives in
  `/friends chronicle` only. A copy taken by a player is not updated; the keeper writes a new copy (from new materials)
  for the lectern.
- **Weddings and births** are noticed by looking at the families' records every few seconds while the couple or the
  baby is loaded, so a wedding is written when they are next seen. The wedding's place ("at the town hall") is a good
  guess: whether the village has a town hall at the time.
- **Birthdays** for those who were already on the team before this update count from the day the update first saw
  them; for everyone after, from the day they joined or were born. Someone away from the camp on their birthday misses
  it that year.
- **Music** needs a note block (or eight planks and a redstone dust) from the chest; without one there is no music that
  evening. It is played straight to everyone's ears rather than by tuning the block for each note, so a note block
  tuned by hand is not changed and nothing redstone hears it.
- **Mourning** applies to friends the village has seen (anyone loaded since the update); someone never loaded since does
  not mourn.
- **Raids** are only noticed while the camp is loaded, and only if one is under way near the camp's centre.
- The Chronicle keeps about 5000 lines (some forty volumes); beyond that the oldest lines are let go from
  `/friends chronicle` (books already written keep theirs).
