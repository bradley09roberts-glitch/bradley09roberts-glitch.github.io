# Kingdoms of the Long Night: World Bible

This is the design canon for the `longnight` mod and its modpack. It describes the whole world and maps lore to systems for future implementation. Ids in `code font` are canon and used verbatim by the code. Everything else may be refined, but changes should be recorded here first.

## 0. Principles

- **Original.** No borrowed names, places or peoples.
- **Layered.** Truth arrives slowly, through common tales, then uncommon guides, then rare histories, then forbidden texts. Books may contradict each other. Some mysteries are never answered.
- **Connected.** Every custom touches a system. A funeral leaves a grave block. A faith changes who may marry. A blight moves grain prices, and grain prices move people.
- **Faith is not a buff.** Religion shapes how people live, marry, bury, trade, judge and rebel. It never adds damage or armour.
- **Human-sized evil.** The cosmic dark feeds on hunger, grief, pride and fear. Most villains are desperate people.
- **Designer's truth** passages are for us. NPCs and books hold only fragments of them.

## 1. The World

### 1.1 Name

The continent is **the Haldmarch**, "the held border". The oldest Valemen songs call the world a march: a borderland held between the Sun above and the Hollow below, with every hearth a watch-fire on that border. Other peoples have their own names for it:

| People | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Eldrin | Aelvaren | "the light that was kept" |
| Dunmar | Grundhald | "the held ground" |
| Aszari | Zahirun | "the lit lands" |
| Ashborn | the Fire's Floor | |
| Burrowfolk | the Wide World | meant as a warning |

Three places lie beyond the Haldmarch. Everyone knows of them and almost no one has seen them:

- **The Hollow** (the End). A rift at the edge of the star-sea, where the Bound Star is chained.
- **The Underfire** (the Nether). A burning underworld, left where something once fell through the floor of the world.
- **The Hush** (the Deep Dark). A silence under the deepest stone that spreads like moss and listens.

### 1.2 Lands and biomes

| Region | Minecraft biomes | People |
|---|---|---|
| The Vale | plains, sunflower plains, forest, birch forest, river valleys | `valemen` |
| The Saltmarch and the Hundred Isles | beach, stony shore, ocean, islands, mangrove fringes | `tidefolk` |
| The Shimmer and the Glass Plain | desert | `aszari` |
| The Starwood | old growth birch, dark forest, flower forest | `eldrin` |
| The Anvil Peaks and the Burned Deep | stony and jagged peaks, windswept hills, deep caves | `dunmar` |
| The Green Combes | meadow, flower plains, cherry grove, gentle hills | `burrowfolk` |
| The Scorch | savanna, savanna plateau, badlands | `ashborn` |
| The Murk | swamp, mangrove swamp, lush and dripstone caves | `skrell` |
| The White Reach | snowy plains and taiga, ice spikes, frozen peaks | frontier, exiles, the ruins of Saramund |

Homeland biomes set starting weights only. A Valemen town in the taiga is normal.

### 1.3 Tone

The motto is "a lantern in every window". The world is vast, old and darker than its people know. Most lives are small: a field, a family, a grudge, a feast. Kings matter less than harvests, until suddenly they matter more. The setting combines deep time and fading glory, a colony sim's sense that a burnt bakery is a tragedy, and a survival game's dread of nightfall. Commoners speak plainly and clergy formally, with no modern idiom. Burrowfolk and skrell supply humour, but the humour never mocks the setting.

## 2. Cosmology

### 2.1 Designer's truth

**The Sun and his sister.** In the beginning there were two lights. **Azhir**, the Sun, ruled the day. **Nahire**, the Dawn-Star and his younger twin, walked the edges of day and night. Beneath creation lay **the Unlit**. It is not evil, and perhaps not alive at all: it is the hunger of nothing to be nothing again. Light is a wound in it, and it wants that wound closed.

**The Kindling Below.** In the Age of Stars the **Starwrights**, an order of Eldrin and human scholars, set out to banish night forever. Their plan was to draw Nahire down and set her burning beneath the world. She fell instead. Her fall burned through the underside of the world, which became the Underfire. It cracked the edge of the sky, which became the Hollow. And it woke the Unlit. The First Night followed.

**The Binding.** The Starwrights, the Eldrin and the Dunmar runesmiths chained the wounded, maddened star in the Hollow. They fed her through the **Lanterns** (end crystals), into which the Starwrights poured their own souls. Her bound, constant light keeps the Unlit asleep. Held too long in the dark, a falling star takes the shape of a dragon. She is jailer and prisoner at once.

**The Ender Dragon is Nahire, the Bound Star.** She is neither evil nor kind. She is in pain, and she guards the Hollow because her chains make her. Killing her ends her pain, and it ends the Binding. Her light goes out, the Unlit stirs, and the Long Night rises.

**The Last Ember.** Her egg is the seed of a new binding. Placing four Lanterns at the exit portal (the vanilla respawn) rekindles a Bound Star and drives back the Night. But a new star is chained, and someone pays the price (section 10).

**Unanswered on purpose.** Designers must never confirm any of these:

- Whether the Unlit has a will. The Choir says it sings. The Watchers say it is mere hunger.
- Who built the Hushed Halls (the "Ones Before").
- Whether the egg, broken under the noon sun, would rise as a second dawn.
- Whether Nahire consented to the Binding.

### 2.4 Layers of revelation

1. **Common.** A wyrm lives at the world's end. The Tall Ones steal stones. Don't stay awake too long. Light a candle for the dead.
2. **Uncommon.** Old magic binds the wyrm. The Sun-church says it ate the Dawn-Star. Omens are rising. Saramund marched away and never returned.
3. **Rare.** The Kindling Below. The Starwrights' fall. The wyrm is a fallen star, and its death would bring a great darkness.
4. **Forbidden.** The wyrm is Nahire. The Lanterns are souls. The Choir seeks the Lightless Door. The rekindling rite has a price.

Book rarity follows these layers. No single text holds the whole truth.

## 3. Deep History

Scholars count years After Binding (AB). The game opens around **2,317 AB**.

### 3.1 The Age of Stars (before reckoning)

- **The Eldrin.** They wake under the stars and raise **Thaevalis**, the Star-Realm. Its capital is **Iriavel**, a city of living trees and amethyst lamps.
- **The Dunmar.** They found **Varnkhar**, the Deep Realm, whose forges are fed by the mountain's heat.
- **The skrell.** They build **Tik-Tazzik, the Clockwarren**, a marsh city of tripwires and moving stone.
- **Ishkarai.** The Glass Kingdom rules the Shimmer from mirror-cities.
- **The great wyrms** rule the high places.
- **The Hushed Halls** are already ancient.
- **The Starwrights** map the star-sea from **the Nine Lenses**, their observatory-towers. They are led by the Archwright **Isthenne**. **Maudrin the Lampwright** warns against the Kindling, and her warning survives as "Maudrin's Dissent".

### 3.2 The Kindling Below and the First Night (about 0 AB)

- **Nahire falls.** Her passing heat melts Ishkarai's mirror-cities. The **Glass Plain** remains: fused sand, with buried streets beneath the dunes.
- **The First Night.** Songs say "a hundred winters"; it was probably twelve years. The dead rose, gate-scars tore open, and hush-moss crept up through the mines.
- **The fall of Saramund.** Saramund was the High Kingdom of the north. Its Tall King **Aldemar** led his people through the Sealed Keeps into the Hollow "to bring the Dawn home". They did not return as men.
- **The Binding.** The Starwrights, the Eldrin and the runesmiths raised the Pale Pillars. The Starwrights gave their souls to the Lanterns, and the order ended.
- **Battlefields:** the **Field of Lanterns** in the White Reach, and the Glass Plain.

### 3.3 The Age of Pillars (1 to 900 AB)

- **The Debt of Light.** The Eldrin houses that gave the most bear fewer children every generation, and Thaevalis dissolves into the **Concord of Houses**.
- **Ostrevane.** The Ostrevane Dominion rises in the Vale (about 300 AB). It builds **the Kingsway**, a stone road with a milestone every league, along with river forts and the Grey Legions.
- **The Cog War** (about 400 AB). The Dunmar destroy the Clockwarren and the skrell scatter. The **Cogsmire** battlefield is still strewn with green copper gears.
- **The fall of Varnkhar** (about 870 AB). The wyrm **Cindermaw** breaks in through the forges. Varnkhar becomes **the Burned Deep**. Its last army pursues the brood into the Underfire and becomes the Charred. The rearguard's stand at **Hammerfall Pass** is the Dunmar's proudest grief.
- **The Burning Summer** (about 880 AB). The Ashborn warleader **Durzha Red-Ember** breaks the southern legions at **the Red Ford**.

### 3.4 The Age of Crowns (900 to 1,900 AB)

- **The Sleepless Plague** (about 920 AB). Its victims could not sleep, and night-mares filled the skies. Ostrevane shatters into the Valemen kingdoms.
- **The Forsworn.** The **Pale Legion** swore to die with the last emperor, **Severan**. When the plague came they broke that oath and bargained with the dark to live. Their descendants are born grey-faced and raid the borders.
- **The Free Ports** (about 1,050 AB). The Saltmarch cities become the Tidefolk republics. **The Great Swell** of 1,212 AB drowns a dozen of them.
- **The Long Walk** (about 1,100 AB). The burrowfolk flee the blighted **Lost Downs**, and a Valemen king grants them the Green Combes in the **Combe Charter**. They pay the rent in honey and candles.
- **The Sunthrones.** The Aszari rebuild atop the dunes and reopen the **Salt Road**.
- **The Burnings** (about 1,400 AB). The fanatic Brands burn hundreds of hearth-witches. A few were guilty.
- **The Year Without Dawn** (1,640 AB). There is no summer at all. The Flamekeeper **Wendreda** loses seven children to the famine and founds the Quiet Abbey, which becomes **the Veiled Choir**. The abbey is burned, but her body is never found. Forbidden texts call her **the Unburied Abbess**.

### 3.5 The Late Age (to the present)

Today's realms are many, small and proud, and they are procedurally generated from the `names` data. Old maps are wrong and every hill has a ruin. Watchers see the Dawn-Star's old place growing dim. Hollowed sightings rise and harvests thin. The player arrives as the founder of a small camp in a fragile peace that history will call **the Lit Years**.

## 4. The Peoples

Each entry covers homeland, building palette, dress (wool and dye colours for clothes and banners), food, crafts, customs, naming, government, war (with a unit roster), magic, rifts, outcasts and unlikely friendships. Law is detailed in section 11.

### 4.1 Valemen (`valemen`, humans)

**Homeland.** The Vale's plains, river meadows and forest edges. They are the most numerous people, and heirs of Ostrevane.

**Building.**
- Timber frames of stripped oak and spruce logs on cobblestone footings, with white terracotta or calcite infill.
- Hay-bale thatch edged with spruce stairs. Cobblestone chimneys with a campfire at the base.
- Lords build in stone bricks and mossy stone bricks.

**Dress.** Undyed wool and brown leather. Green and red on feast days. Nobles wear house colours. Flamekeepers wear grey with an orange stole.

**Food and crafts.** Bread, mutton stew, pease pottage, apples, cider and ale, and pies at Reaping. Wool cloth, smithing, wheelwrighting, yew longbows, bell-founding, illuminated books.

**Customs.**
- Hearth-joining weddings.
- Harvest-home feasts.
- Highsun tournaments.
- Beating the parish bounds in Brightwake.
- A lantern in the window on the longest night.

**Names.** Old-Vale given names (Haldric, Aldith) with place or trade surnames (Ashwick, Thatchwell). Nobles add "of {seat}".

**Government.** Hereditary kingdoms. Kings rule over Earls, Barons and Knights. Villages elect Reeves.

**War.** Knights and men-at-arms behind yeoman archers. Roster: Levy Spearman, Billman, Yeoman Archer, Man-at-Arms, Knight, Outrider (scout), Sergeant, Siegewright.

**Magic.** Hearth-witches are quietly relied upon. Court astrologers come from the Lantern Colleges. Witch-hunts break out in bad years.

**Rifts.**
- Old Blood nobles against New Coin burghers.
- Kingsway Restorationists, who dream of a reborn Ostrevane, against proud local lords.
- The Brands against everyone they suspect.

**Outcasts.** Greenwood outlaws, oathbreakers, unpaid ex-soldiers, unwed mothers turned out of strict parishes.

**Unlikely friendships.** Burrowfolk brewers. Ashborn mercenary companies. Skrell rat-catchers tolerated by parish priests.

### 4.2 Tidefolk (`tidefolk`, humans)

**Homeland.** The Saltmarch coasts, the Hundred Isles and river mouths. Their Free Ports are merchant republics.

**Building.**
- Stone-brick quays.
- Tall narrow houses of spruce and mangrove planks with blue and cyan terracotta trim.
- Chains as rigging, barrels and nets.
- Prismarine and sea lanterns for the rich.
- Bell-houses with a bell block, and stone-brick lighthouses.

**Dress.** Blue, light blue and white. Captains wear cyan sashes. Widows wear grey-blue. Tide-Wives wear sea-green with shells.

**Food and crafts.** Fish stew, kelp bread, salt fish, seaweed wine. Ships, rope, salt, maps, glass, dyes.

**Customs.** The Blessing of the Boats. Net-binding weddings. Oaths sworn on salt. Never whistle at sea. A storm candle for every sailor.

**Names.** Lilting names ending in vowels (Davro, Marella) with ship and harbour surnames (Velaro, Ancoretto).

**Government.** Merchant republics. The **Helm** is elected by the **Council of Hulls**. Harbourmasters and guilds hold real power.

**War.** Navy first. Marines, crossbows and hired pikes. Roster: Marine, Arbalester, Harpooner, Boarding Axe, Pike Mercenary, Captain, Navigator (scout).

**Magic.** Pragmatic. They pay tide-singers and apothecaries and ask no questions.

**Rifts.** Old Hulls against New Sails. Smugglers against Customs. Mother's Mercy against the Hungry Tide.

**Outcasts.** Wreckers, mutineers, debt-bonded rowers.

**Unlikely friendships.** Aszari caravan-lords. Skrell salvage-divers. Dunmar, who need ships and hate every minute aboard.

### 4.3 Aszari (`aszari`, humans)

**Homeland.** The Shimmer's oases and river valleys, rebuilt atop buried Ishkarai.

**Building.**
- Sandstone in all its forms: cut, smooth and chiseled friezes.
- Red sandstone accents, orange, yellow and white terracotta.
- Smooth-sandstone domes. Sunhouses with gold trim and an open oculus.
- Water channels in courtyards, wool awnings, glass mirrors.
- **Waterhouses**: public wells with a cauldron.

**Dress.** White and cream robes, saffron and orange, gold jewellery. Priests wear white and gold. Caravan-lords wear deep blue.

**Food and crafts.** Flatbread, spiced rabbit, dates (sweet berries), melon, honey pastries, "dawnbrew". Glass, mirrors, alchemy, perfume, carpets, gold-work, camels.

**Customs.** Dawn prayers. Contracts only in daylight. Water is never refused. Three days of guest-sanctuary. Poetry contests at the Day Without Shadow.

**Names.** Given names with z, sh, q and kh (Tazim, Ilsara). Patronymics: "sar-" for a son, "sari-" for a daughter, as in Tazim sar-Qadrun. Noble lineages also keep house names.

**Government.** Theocratic city-monarchies. A **Qadhir** must be confirmed "sun-touched" by the priesthood. Vizars and **Sunjudges** serve under the Qadhir. Cities swear to a Sunthrone.

**War.** Mobile and hard-hitting. Roster: Dune Rider (horse archer), Sun-Lancer, Slinger, Mirror Guard (temple elite), Firepot Thrower, Caravan Sword, Pathfinder (scout).

**Magic.** Alchemy is respected. Star-reading is suspect. Sorcery after dark is illegal.

**Rifts.** Orthodox Unfading against mystic Twin-Dawners. City-lords against caravan-lords. The zealous Burning Mirror.

**Outcasts.** Night-workers, water-thieves, the "shadow-touched" born in eclipses.

**Unlikely friendships.** Tidefolk merchants. Dunmar copper-traders. Ashborn horse-breeders.

### 4.4 Eldrin (`eldrin`, elves)

**Homeland.** The Starwood's old birch and dark-oak forests. They are long-lived (about 400 years), few and declining.

**Building.**
- Living birch and dark-oak arches and tree flets.
- Moss, flowering azalea and glow lichen.
- Calcite and quartz halls, amethyst "star-glass".
- Froglights or glowstone in lantern-cages. End rods ("star-rods") are relics.
- Hilltop observatories. Almost no cut stone.

**Dress.** Silver-grey, deep blue and forest green. White for mourning. House colours are embroidered as stars.

**Food and crafts.** Glow berries, mushrooms, honeyed starbread, elderflower wine, little meat. Bows, healing potions, star-charts, woodcarving, silverwork.

**Customs.**
- Naming Nights under the birth-stars.
- The Long Remembering of house histories through Deepnight.
- A tree-debt: one tree planted for every tree felled in your name.

**Names.** Flowing given names (Ivaren, Ilyra) with house names (Ilvareth).

**Government.** The **Concord of Houses**. Each house sends its Eldest, and the Eldest elect a **First Voice** for a 21-year star-cycle. They govern by slow consensus.

**War.** Few soldiers, all superb, who ambush and vanish. Roster: Sentinel Archer, Warden (scout), Glaive-Dancer, Star-Singer (rare battle mage), Elder Blade.

**Magic.** Druidry and star-lore come naturally. They fear excessive star-magic because they remember its price.

**Rifts.** **Dimming Houses** want to withdraw into dream. **Waking Houses** want to engage with the world. **House Maervaine** guards the Binding's full secret.

**Outcasts.** Wanderers who leave for human cities. Tree-debt breakers. Half-Eldrin children.

**Unlikely friendships.** Burrowfolk, who feed them. Dunmar runesmiths, an old Binding comradeship. Skrell, which is scandalous.

### 4.5 Dunmar (`dunmar`, dwarves)

**Homeland.** The Anvil Peaks and the deep caves. They mourn Varnkhar, which is still dragon-held.

**Building.**
- Deepslate bricks and tiles, stone bricks, tuff.
- Basalt pillars, chiseled-deepslate rune panels, iron doors and bars.
- Oxidized-copper gatehouse roofs. Blast furnaces and lava forges. Chained lanterns.
- Gold only in ancestor halls.
- Straight halls, square doors, thick walls.

**Dress.** Charcoal, black and iron-grey, with braided red or ochre clan cords. Priests wear undyed grey with chains of name-tokens.

**Food and crafts.** Mushroom stew, roast goat, black bread, moss-brewed dark beer. Mining, smithing, runes, stonecutting, pistons and rails, clockwork locks.

**Customs.** Name-returning (children receive ancestors' names). Weigh-ledgers of every wrong and debt. Marriage by ancestor-lot. Shaving the beard as mourning.

**Names.** Hard consonants (Bardek, Dagra). Clan names are compounds (Ironvein) or in the old tongue (Grumhalm).

**Government.** A **Hold-King** or Hold-Queen rules over Clan-Fathers and Clan-Mothers, Forge-Thanes, Shield-Thanes and the **Lawspeaker of Stone**.

**War.** Disciplined heavy infantry and engineers. Roster: Shieldbearer, Axe-Thane, Bolter, Sapper, Runeguard, Ironclad, Delver (scout).

**Magic.** Runes only. Uncut "loose magic" is distrusted.

**Rifts.**
- The Unbroken Line (blood-purists) against reformers.
- **Returners**, who would retake Varnkhar at any cost, against **Keepers**.
- The forge-guilds against the clan-kings.

**Outcasts.** The **Unnamed**, struck from the hall stones. Surface-dwellers. Unbalanced debtors.

**Unlikely friendships.** Eldrin. Tidefolk shippers. Skrell tinkers, whom some holds employ while everyone pretends otherwise.

### 4.6 Burrowfolk (`burrowfolk`, small hill-folk)

**Homeland.** The Green Combes: meadows, gentle hills, cherry groves. They are about four feet tall, practical, sociable and stubborn, and not as harmless as they look.

**Building.**
- Hillside homes under grass and moss mounds.
- Round doors framed with spruce or oak trapdoors.
- Mud-brick chimneys.
- Plank interiors full of barrels, flower pots, candles and recipe books.
- Beehives, gardens, hedges, sweet-berry bushes and cherry trees.

**Dress.** Mustard, moss green, rust orange and brown. Waistcoats with brass buttons. A ribbon of office for moot-folk.

**Food and crafts.** Pies, potatoes, carrots, honey, cakes, cheese, mead and brown ale, and four meals a day on principle. Brewing, beekeeping and **candle-making** (their beeswax lights half the Haldmarch's funerals), preserving, record-keeping.

**Customs.**
- The Bee-Waking: whispering the year's news to the hives.
- Seasonal moots.
- Giving gifts on one's own birthday.
- "Shut the door and keep the kettle on" is both proverb and famine law.

**Names.** Soft, cosy given names (Tobble, Tansy) with homely surnames (Hivewright, Kettlebrook).

**Government.** Moots of all householders, with these elected roles:
- The **Moot-Mother** or **Moot-Father**.
- The Bailiff of the Combe.
- Hedgewardens.
- The Keeper of the Common Pot, the famine store.

**War.** Cunning militia that fights from hedges and pits. Roster: Slinger, Hedgewarden (short bow, scout), Pitchfork Militia, Beekeeper (throws hives), Trapper, Pony Rider.

**Magic.** "Kettle-charms are fine. Wizards are trouble." Burrowfolk are strangely hard to curse.

**Rifts.** Stay-at-Homes against Wanderers. Old families against newcomers. Hivewright against Kettlebrook, for reasons nobody remembers.

**Outcasts.** "Over-the-hill" exiles (famine hoarders) and adventurers, who are not quite respectable.

**Unlikely friendships.** Eldrin. Ashborn trader clans, who adore burrowfolk ale. Valemen soldiers once hidden in a burrow during a war.

### 4.7 Ashborn (`ashborn`, orcs)

**Homeland.** The Scorch's red savanna, mesas and badlands. They are big, long-memoried and clan-proud. They are **not** uniformly hostile: some clans raid, some trade, and many sell their swords.

**Building.**
- Acacia, red sandstone, and terracotta in red, brown, black and orange.
- Hide tents of brown and red wool for clans on the move.
- Acacia-fence palisades with pointed dripstone.
- Bone-block totems with skulls.
- A central ember-pit of magma or a campfire ringed with basalt.
- Dark-oak long-halls for settled clans.

**Dress.** Ochre, red, black and brown. Ash-grey war-paint. Earned scars and bone tokens.

**Food and crafts.** Roast meat, blood sausage, stone-baked flatbread, fermented mare's milk. Leather, bone-carving, terracotta, badland gold, war-drums. The finest horses in the Haldmarch.

**Customs.** First Blood hunts. Names are earned. Guest-right is absolute. Feuds can be settled by champion. Marriages between clans seal alliances.

**Names.** Guttural names (Gorrask, Mazhra) with a translated clan name (Emberfang).

**Government.** Clans are led by an **Ukhar** (chief), confirmed by the **Old Mothers**. In great need, the **Ash-Moot** raises a **Gor-Ukhar**, a high chief.

**War.** Shock and speed. Roster: Raider, Javelineer, Tusk-Rider (boar or great-horse cavalry), Ember-Berserker, Wolf-Runner (scout), Shaman, Warlord. Mercenary companies such as **the Iron Tusks** sell this roster for coin.

**Magic.** Ember-shamans are revered. Book-magic is "cold fire" and is distrusted.

**Rifts.** Red Ember (war) clans against Cold Ember (trade) clans. Coin-loyal mercenary companies. The Ashmaw death-cult.

**Outcasts.** The **Emberless**, cast out or with a dead clan fire. Half-Ashborn children of mercenaries.

**Unlikely friendships.** Burrowfolk brewers. Valemen border lords. Aszari horse-buyers. Dunmar, who admire Ashborn bone-work only when drunk.

### 4.8 Skrell (`skrell`, goblins)

**Homeland.** The Murk's swamps, mangroves and caves, and the drains under human towns. They are small, quick, clever and short-lived (about 40 years). They are scavengers, tinkerers and traders, and often bandits.

**Building.**
- Mud and mud brick on mangrove roots.
- Scaffolding and ladders.
- Mismatched salvage: cracked bricks, mossy cobblestone, oxidized copper, stolen doors.
- Chains, cobwebs, mushroom blocks, lanterns on poles.
- Working redstone traps: tripwire, dispensers, trapdoor pits.

**Dress.** Patchwork green, brown and grey, with stolen bright scraps worn proudly. Glass goggles. Tool belts.

**Food and crafts.** Anything: mushrooms, frogs, slime-jelly, and "skrell stew". A weakness for burrowfolk pie. Sparkwork (redstone), traps, locks, boom-dust (gunpowder), salvage, rat-catching, fencing stolen goods.

**Customs.**
- **Shiny-Law**: finders keep only if they share.
- The **Rattle**, a bone-and-bell staff, makes its holder chief.
- Interrupting a story is polite.
- Debts are paid in favours.
- The dead keep their favourite shiny thing.

**Names.** Short, clicky names (Zikka, Snikk) with place-names for families (Drainmouth).

**Government.** A **Rattle-King** or Rattle-Queen, Tinkbosses, and Gutter-Councils in human towns.

**War.** Ambush and dirty tricks. Roster: Cutpurse, Dartthrower, Trapper, Boom-Tosser, Tinkboss, Bog-Rider (giant frogs), Sneak (scout).

**Magic.** They love it and steal it. Sparkwork looks like witchcraft. They say it is "just being clever".

**Rifts.** Bandit tribes against trader tribes. Town-skrell (the Guild of Small Hands) against marsh-skrell. **The Old Cog**, who want the Clockwarren rebuilt and the Dunmar punished.

**Outcasts.** Town-skrell are outcasts twice over: distrusted by humans and called soft by marsh kin.

**Unlikely friendships.** Eldrin scholars. Burrowfolk cooks. Tidefolk salvage crews. Lonely Dunmar tinkers.

## 5. The Faiths

Each person has one faith id. Each culture has a default faith, but people may follow another: a Valemen sailor may keep the Tide. Faith shapes daily life, law, marriage, burial and charity. It is never a combat bonus.

| Faith id | Name | Main followers |
|---|---|---|
| `hearth` | the Kindled Hearth | Valemen, burrowfolk, town-skrell |
| `sun` | the Unfading Sun | Aszari, some Tidefolk and Valemen nobles |
| `tide` | the Drowned Mother | Tidefolk, coastal Valemen |
| `grove` | the Old Grove | Eldrin, rural Valemen, burrowfolk |
| `ancestors` | the Halls of the Forefathers | Dunmar, northern Valemen |
| `stars` | the Watchers of the Star-Sea | Eldrin, scholars, Aszari dissidents |
| `ember` | the Ember Ancestors | Ashborn |
| `veiled_choir` | the Veiled Choir (forbidden) | the desperate, everywhere, in secret |

### 5.1 The Kindled Hearth (`hearth`)

**Belief.** Every true fire descends from the First Flame the Sun gave the first family. **Hearthkin** (household spirits, seen as allays) dwell where a fire is kept honestly. The remembrance of the living warms the dead. A cold, forgotten grave lets them wander.

**Clergy.** **Flamekeepers** are parish priests and may marry. **Kindlers** are deacons. The **Keeper of the First Flame** presides at the **Hearth of Hearths**. Village hearth-witches are not clergy, which is a matter of endless argument.

**Temples.** **Kindlehalls** are stone halls with an undying central fire (a campfire on a stone-brick dais), a candle-wall for the dead, and an almonry. Every home keeps a candle niche by the fireplace.

**Festivals.**
- **Rekindling** (1 Brightwake). Every fire is put out and relit from the Kindlehall.
- **Last Sheaf** (Reaping).
- **The Lantern Vigil** (the longest night of Deepnight). A light in every window and open doors.

**Pilgrimage.** To the Hearth of Hearths, carrying a lantern lit at home and bringing back a coal.

**Burial: "the Last Candle".**
- The body is buried in the parish yard under a coarse-dirt mound, with a cobblestone-wall or stone-brick-wall headstone, a sign epitaph, and a **candle** on top.
- Kin relight the candle on each death-day and at the Vigil.
- A grave left unlit for a year becomes **unremembered** (section 13).

**Charity.**
- **Bowl-Right**: one night by a Kindlehall fire and one bowl for any traveller.
- Almshouses.
- The Common Hearth fund.

**Schisms and fanatics.** The **Ashen Reform** would share the flame only with the faithful. **The Brands** are witch-hunters who burn hearth-witches and suspected Choir members, often wrongly.

**Forbidden.**
- Letting your fire die on the longest night.
- **Cold-hearting**: putting out another family's fire.
- Stealing grave candles.

**Daily life.**
- **Hearth-joining** weddings. Divorce is "splitting the ashes".
- Flamekeepers keep the **Hearth-rolls** of births, marriages and deaths (the in-world genealogy).
- The hearth-tax is levied per household fire.

### 5.2 The Unfading Sun (`sun`)

**Belief.** Azhir, the Sun, is truth, law and life. What is done in light is honest, and what is done in darkness is suspect. His twin Nahire was devoured by the Star-Eater. The orthodox hold that Azhir is enough. Mystics wait for Nahire's return.

**Clergy.** The **Radiant** (priests and priestesses). **Sun-Readers** keep the calendar. **Sunjudges** sit in law. The **Mehira**, high priestess of each Sunthrone, confirms rulers as sun-touched.

**Temples.** **Sunhouses** are sandstone domes whose oculus throws a noon beam onto a gold altar. Mirror-towers light dark streets. Every home has an east-facing mirror.

**Festivals.**
- **Dawnfeast** (1 Brightwake).
- **The Day Without Shadow** (Highsun). Rulers are confirmed, contracts renewed, poets compete.
- **Nahire's Mourning** (Deepnight). Fasting and veiled mirrors.

**Pilgrimage.** To walk the **Glass Plain** barefoot at noon.

**Burial: "Sun-sealing".**
- The body is sun-dried on a rooftop bier for a day, then sealed in an east-facing tomb.
- The grave is a smooth-sandstone slab with a chiseled-sandstone headstone on its east end and a sunflower or dandelion at the foot.
- Graves facing away from dawn breed husks.

**Charity.** **Waterhouses**: no one is refused water under the sun. Three days of guest-sanctuary. Noon alms.

**Schisms and fanatics.** **Twin-Dawners** are secretive mystics who believe Nahire can rise again. **The Burning Mirror** are zealots who want the Star-Eater slain at any cost and who burn "shadow-lovers".

**Forbidden.** Sorcery after dark. Covering a sun-altar. Eclipse rites. Night contracts, which are void.

**Daily life.** Work starts at dawn and trade stops at dusk. Weddings are blessed at noon, and an outsider spouse takes "the first light" (conversion). A ruler without sun-touch loses legitimacy.

### 5.3 The Drowned Mother (`tide`)

**Belief.** The sea is the Mother. She gives fish and takes sailors, and both are love. She once lost a child of light that fell from the sky, and she wept the oceans. The drowned are her children. Those not given to her properly return hungry: the unblessed, seen as drowned.

**Clergy.** **Tide-Wives** are priestesses, often widows. **Salt-Priests** are men, often old sailors. **Bell-Ringers** sound tides and storms.

**Temples.** Harbour **Bell-houses**: stone towers with a bell block, prismarine and sea-pickle mosaics, and a wall of votive boats. Cliff-shrines of shell and driftwood.

**Festivals.**
- **Blessing of the Boats** (Blossomtide).
- **Calm Day** (Highsun). No sailing, and debts forgiven.
- **The Drowned Remembrance** (Leaffall). Floating lanterns for the lost.

**Pilgrimage.** To drop a carved token on the **Weeping Reef**. The **Mother's Throats** (ocean monuments) are holiest, and entering them is sacrilege.

**Burial: "Giving to the Mother".**
- The body is wrapped in sailcloth, weighted, and given to the sea from a boat or a cliff called the **Drop**.
- Those who die inland get a **salt-cairn** facing the sea: a cobblestone or gravel cairn, a nautilus shell in an item frame, and a sea pickle on top.

**Charity.** **The Widows' Net** fund. Harbour hospices. Every ship must answer a distress bell.

**Schisms and fanatics.** **Mother's Mercy** (orthodox) against **the Hungry Tide**, who say the sea must be fed and have drowned "offerings" in bad years.

**Forbidden.** Whistling at sea. Killing dolphins. Entering a Mother's Throat. Burying a sailor in earth.

**Daily life.** **Net-binding** weddings. Oaths on salt. The Tide-Wives' weather almanac. Voyage tithes. Children born at sea are "Mother's own" and may never be refused a ship.

### 5.4 The Old Grove (`grove`)

**Belief.** Life is one cycle: seed, growth, fall, rot and seed again. Death returns us to the roots. Breaking the cycle is the gravest sin, whether by waste, by burning forests, or above all by undeath.

**Clergy.** **Grovewardens** are druids who teach, bless fields and judge boundaries. **Seedbearers** are wandering priests with seed-stores. **Thornsisters** are hermits of the wild.

**Temples.** **Ring-groves**: a ring of oaks around a mossy clearing, with mossy-cobblestone standing stones and a **Crowned Oak** (the region's oldest tree) at the centre. Wayside shrines are a stone and a flower pot.

**Festivals.**
- **Greenvow** (Seedtide). First planting and renewed vows.
- **Last Sheaf** (Reaping). A sheaf-figure is kept until spring.
- **Leaf-Wake** (Leaffall). The dead are named aloud under the trees.

**Pilgrimage.** To plant a seed from home in the **Thousand-Year Wood**.

**Burial: "Tree-burial".**
- The body is buried in a shroud under a new sapling: oak for most, birch for Eldrin, cherry for burrowfolk.
- The grave is the sapling on podzol or rooted dirt with a mossy-cobblestone marker.
- Graveyards grow into groves. Cutting a grave-tree is a crime against memory.

**Charity.** **Gleaning-Right** after harvest. Seed-banks opened in famine.

**Schisms and fanatics.** **The Thornbound** sabotage farms and burn sawmills. **The Rotkin** are a heresy that worships decay and slides toward the Rotwood.

**Forbidden.** Felling a Crowned Oak. Setting forest fires. Necromancy. Felling in Seedtide without leave.

**Daily life.** Couples plant a **marriage-tree**. Woodcutters ask leave and replant. Lords who clear forests lose Grovewarden blessings, including at their own funerals.

### 5.5 The Halls of the Forefathers (`ancestors`)

**Belief.** The dead live on in memory, carved in stone and spoken in halls. A person is their name, their deeds and their debts. The ancestors judge and, through the lot, advise. To be forgotten is the only true death. To be woken is worse.

**Clergy.** **Hallkeepers** keep halls and genealogies. **Graven Priests** carve the name-stones. **Lot-Casters** read the ancestor-lot.

**Temples.** **Ancestor Halls**: pillared deepslate-brick halls lined with sealed niches, a basalt-pillar row per clan, and an iron brazier. Northern Valemen build small stone **name-chapels**.

**Festivals.**
- **First Forging** (Brightwake).
- **Hall Night** (Leaffall). Every name is read from dusk to dawn, and no one may leave.
- **Debt-Day** (Dimming). Ledgers are balanced and feuds settled.

**Pilgrimage.** To touch the dragon-held **Gate of Varnkhar** and leave a name-token.

**Burial: "Stone-sealing".**
- The body goes into a one-by-two niche in stone or deepslate bricks, sealed with chiseled deepslate or chiseled stone bricks.
- A sign bears the name and the person's deeds.
- Surface folk raise a stone-brick cairn with a carved headstone.

**Charity.** **The Clan Purse**: no one goes hungry while the clan eats. Orphans go to the nearest kin hall.

**Schisms and fanatics.** **The Forgetters** are heretics who say the dead should be let go. **The Unbroken Line** are blood-purists who forbid marriage outside the clan.

**Forbidden.** **Unnaming** (defacing a name-stone), the worst crime. Opening a sealed niche. Waking the dead.

**Daily life.** Children are given ancestors' names. Marriages need a favourable lot. Rulers display lineage-stones as legitimacy.

### 5.6 The Watchers of the Star-Sea (`stars`)

**Belief.** The stars are lamps of the first watchers. They record what was and warn of what will be. Night is not evil: it is the time to see. The Watchers half-remember the Fall and the Binding, and they hold that the Falling Light must never be slain.

**Clergy.** **Watchers** observe. **Seers** prophesy and are often half-mad. **Lantern-scholars** teach.

**Temples.** **Star-domes**: open-roofed hilltop observatories with calcite floors inlaid with amethyst constellations, a spyglass stand, chart lecterns, and a lightning rod used as a gnomon.

**Festivals.**
- **The Night of Falling Lights** (Reaping).
- **The Vigil of Watchers** (Dimming). The year's prophecies are read.
- **Star-Naming** of newborns.

**Pilgrimage.** To **the Broken Lantern**, the first Starwright tower.

**Burial: "Under the open sky".**
- The dead lie face-up in a roofless hilltop tomb: a stone-slab bier with a lantern or end rod on a short pillar and the epitaph on a sign.
- Eldrin may instead scatter ashes from a height.

**Charity.** Free **Lantern Schools** teach any child letters. Free almanacs for farmers.

**Schisms and fanatics.** **Fatalists** say prophecy is fixed. **The Hopeful** say it is a warning. **The New Kindlers** would pull down another star to fight the Night.

**Forbidden.** Star-calling. Reading the **Black Charts** (maps of the Hollow). Teaching the Kindling.

**Daily life.** Birth-charts decide names and lucky days. Marriages need compatible charts, though a gift can "correct" them. Omen-born children are watched. Courts keep Watchers as advisors.

### 5.7 The Ember Ancestors (`ember`)

**Belief.** Every Ashborn carries an ember of the first fire. At death it returns to the clan fire, and the greatest go down to the **Ember Below** (the Underfire) to burn with the war-ancestors. A clan lives as long as its ember burns.

**Clergy.** **Ember-Mothers** (the Old Mothers) are shamans and judges. **Firetalkers** are male war-shamans. **Ember-Bearers** carry the clan ember in a clay pot on the march.

**Temples.** **Ember-pits** in every camp: magma block or a soul campfire, ringed with basalt and bone-block totems topped with skulls. Every household keeps a coal.

**Festivals.**
- **First Blood** (Brightwake).
- **The Ash-Moot** (Highsun). Disputes, trade and marriages between clans.
- **The Great Burning** (Leaffall). The names of the dead are shouted into the flames.

**Pilgrimage.** To the **Mouth of Fire**, a basalt volcano. The boldest go on through a gate-scar to "visit the grandfathers".

**Burial: "the Pyre".**
- Cremation on acacia. The ashes are mixed into the clan ember and into kin war-paint.
- The memorial is a cobblestone ring around an unlit campfire, with an upright bone block and a skull for warriors.

**Charity.** **The Shared Kill**: every hunter must share. **Ember-children**: orphans are raised by the whole clan.

**Schisms and fanatics.** **Red Ember** (the ancestors want glory) against **Cold Ember** (the ancestors want prosperity). **The Ashmaw** are a death-seeking berserker cult.

**Forbidden.** Letting the clan ember die. Killing a guest. **Ember-eating**, the devouring of another's soul for power.

**Daily life.** Marriages bind clans, and the spouse joins the larger fire. Adult names are earned. Chiefs need the Old Mothers' confirmation. Mercenary contracts are sworn over the ember and are therefore holy.

### 5.8 The Veiled Choir (`veiled_choir`, forbidden)

**Outer teaching.** Before light there was the Quiet: no hunger, grief or death. Light is a wound, and the Jailer keeps the world bleeding. When the Jailer falls, the faithful will sing the world back to peace and rule the gentle dark undying.

**Inner truth.** The Unlit grants power for souls. Liches who sold theirs long ago lead the Choir. They want the Night because in the Night they are kings.

**Ranks.**
- **Choristers**: the grieving, starving and dying.
- **Cantors**: necromancers.
- **the Veiled**: masked cell leaders.
- **Choirmasters**: liches. The first was the Unburied Abbess.

**Temples.** Hidden **hush-chapels** in crypts and Hushed Halls: black candles, soul lanterns, transplanted hush-moss, a ring of skulls and an empty frame.

**Festivals.** **The Hollow Moon** (each new moon). **The Unlit Mass** (the longest night, while the world keeps the Lantern Vigil).

**Burial.** None. "The dead are kept" in unmarked open pits, ready for raising.

**The lure.** **The Quiet Table**: soup kitchens that appear in famine, run by kind strangers.

**Signs of a cell.**
- Black candles bought in bulk.
- Missing corpses.
- Hush-moss out of place.
- Townsfolk who stop dreaming.
- Night-mares avoiding one house.

**Countering it.** Feed the hungry first, keep graves remembered, gather evidence, and hold a trial.

## 6. The Calendar

The common reckoning has **eight months, two per season**. The default is **4 in-game days per month**, a 32-day year (configurable). In every month:
- **Day 1 is Fairday**: markets, rents and hiring fairs.
- **The last day is Moot-day**: courts and councils sit.

Holy days fall on Fairday unless the table says otherwise. Peoples also keep their own month names (the Dunmar name months for metals, the Eldrin for constellations), but trade uses the common ones.

| # | Month | Season | Life of the land | Holy days and events |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brightwake** | early spring | Ploughing, lambing, Reeves elected, winter debts due | Rekindling (hearth), Dawnfeast (sun), First Forging (ancestors), First Blood (ember) |
| 2 | **Seedtide** | spring | Sowing, hiring fairs, bees wake, armies muster | Greenvow (grove), Bee-Waking (burrowfolk) |
| 3 | **Blossomtide** | late spring | Weddings and dances, fleets and caravans set out | Blessing of the Boats (tide), Naming Nights (eldrin) |
| 4 | **Highsun** | midsummer | Great fairs, the **Grand Tourney**, haymaking, campaign season | Day Without Shadow (sun), Calm Day (tide), Ash-Moot (ember), Midsummer Moot (burrowfolk) |
| 5 | **Reaping** | late summer | Harvest, taxes and tithes collected, prices fall | Last Sheaf (hearth, grove), Night of Falling Lights (stars) |
| 6 | **Leaffall** | autumn | Slaughter, salting, brewing, cellar-stocking. Raiders target full barns. | Hall Night (ancestors), Great Burning (ember), Drowned Remembrance (tide), Leaf-Wake (grove) |
| 7 | **Dimming** | early winter | Firewood, assizes and trials, peace talks, roads close | Vigil of Watchers (stars), Debt-Day (ancestors), Cellar Moot (burrowfolk) |
| 8 | **Deepnight** | midwinter | Hunger month: tales, repairs, the most deaths | Lantern Vigil (hearth), Nahire's Mourning (sun), Long Remembering (eldrin), Unlit Mass (choir, secret) |

**The economic year.**
- Grain is cheapest in Reaping and dearest in late Deepnight.
- Winter preparation (wood, salt, preserved food) is a settlement task in Leaffall and Dimming.
- War declarations cluster in Seedtide and Highsun. Peace talks cluster in Dimming.

## 7. Magic Traditions

Magic is real, rare, costly and social. Every tradition has teachers, a price and a reputation.

**The lost art.** The Starwrights joined star-wizardry and runesmithing to something now lost: **soul-lanterning**, storing a soul in crystal. It survives only in forbidden texts, and the Choir covets it.

## 8. Dark Powers

Every dark power has an understandable cost (usually paid by someone else), visible signs, and countermeasures that ordinary people can attempt. All of them grow stronger as the Long Night advances.

| Power | What it is | Cost | Signs | Countermeasures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Necromancy** (grave-calling) | Raising the dead as servants. Practised by Choir Cantors and Forsworn. | Each raising spends the memory of the dead, so their kin begin to forget them. The caster stops dreaming. | Disturbed graves, missing bodies, unlit grave candles, cold spots, dogs refusing a road, skeletons in ranks | Remembered graves. Proper rites per faith. Burning the bodies. Finding the caster. |
| **Curses** (the black thread) | A grudge given shape. It strikes crops, health, luck or love. | The curser must keep the hate alive, and a broken curse rebounds on them. | Knotted black thread on a doorframe, soured milk, lame livestock, a run of small accidents | Threshold wards. Reconciliation (a resolved grudge breaks the curse). Burning the thread in a holy fire. |
| **Haunted objects** (grief-bound things) | Items tied to an unresolved death. | Grief chose it, not a person. | Cold to the touch, whispering, returning to one place, nightmares in the house | Return it to the grave. Right the wrong. A priest's rite. |
| **Forbidden rituals** | The Unlit Mass, star-calling, opening the Lightless Door, ember-eating. | Lives, souls, sanity, sometimes a village. | Hollow Moon gatherings, black-candle circles, hush-moss, sudden total silence | Interrupt the rite. Destroy the focus. Bring light. |
| **Soul bargains** (the Quiet Contract) | Power, life or a lost one returned, paid for with a soul. ## 9. Dragons and Great Beasts

### 9.1 The great wyrms

The great wyrms are older than any crown, and a handful remain. A realm in a wyrm's range has three choices:
- **Pay tribute**: livestock left at a marked stone.
- **Call a Wyrmhunt**: glory, death, and claims on crowns.
- **Hide.**

| Wyrm | Territory and lair | Behaviour | Warning signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cindermaw** (red) | The Burned Deep, asleep on Varnkhar's gold | Sleeps for decades, wakes hungry and burns villages within two days' flight | Hot springs boiling, sulphur winds, smoke from old shafts, goats fleeing downhill, hold-gold growing warm |
| **Rimeback** (white) | An ice-cavern in the White Reach | Winter hunter. Takes herds and freezes rivers to cross them. Hates fire. | Summer frost, deer frozen standing, windless snowfall, ice-scarred cliffs |
| **Sorrowscale, the Mire-Mother** | A drowned temple in the Murk | Raises fen-drake broods. Some skrell tend her eggs in return for being spared. | Frogs silent, bogs bubbling at night, eggs in the reeds, livestock gone at dusk, skrell suddenly polite |
| **Ashkaruun the Dune-Sleeper** | Under the Shimmer's great dunes | Wakes in drought and swallows caravans whole | Dunes moving against the wind, wells failing, camels refusing to walk, sand trembling at noon |
| **Old Saltwound** (sea-drake) | Saltmarch sea-caves | Rides storms and sinks ships. Tidefolk sometimes pay it in cargo. | Gulls flying inland, dead fish on the tide, ships overdue, green lightning |

**Lesser drakes.** Fen-drakes, sky-drakes and fire-drakes, from dog-sized to horse-sized. They are the everyday dragon problem: stolen sheep, burned ricks, a child taken. Ashborn hunt them for glory and Dunmar for their scales.

**Wyrm politics.**
- A slain wyrm's hoard is an instant war-cause.
- Paying tribute looks weak.
- A wyrm-slayer becomes a contender for crowns.
- Warning signs should feed `dragon_sighting` rumours days before an attack.

### 9.2 Other great beasts

- **Stonebacks** (cave trolls). They sleep like rocks by day and break into cellars from below at night.
- **Duskhounds.** Pony-sized wolves that hunt in deep winter and in the Long Night. Signs: howling by day, and dogs whining at doors.
- **Weavers.** Giant spiders of the Rotwood. Signs: webbed paths and cocooned deer.
- **Cairnwalkers.** Hill-giants that are harmless unless their cairns are disturbed.
- **The Hushwarden.** Blind and unstoppable. Dunmar children learn: "In the deep quiet, be quieter."
- **The Mother's Eyes.** They guard the Mother's Throats.
- **The Herald.** Comes only through a Choir rite. When it comes, a realm burns.
- **Forsworn war-beasts** (ravagers). Signs: crops trampled in straight lines and bellowing at night.

## 10. The Long Night

### 10.1 Overview

The world stores a **Night Stage** (0 to 5) and a hidden **Hush** meter (0 to 100) that measures the Choir's progress.

- **The Hush rises** with unremembered graves, famine deaths, hidden Choir cells, open gate-scars and spreading hush-moss.
- **The Hush falls** with tended graves, fed refugees, exposed cells and cleansed Rotwood.
- **Time alone never pushes the stage past 2.** Only the Dragon's death or the Choir's success does that.

### 10.2 Stages

**Stage 0: The Lit Years.** The default world, with rumours of the Hollow Wyrm and the Tall Ones.

**Stage 1: Omens.**

Trigger: world day 30 or later, or the first Seeker's Eye is thrown.

Effects:
- Falling stars on clear nights.
- More Hollowed seen.
- The Watchers' prophecy circulates (`star_prophecy` becomes uncommon).
- Children are born **omen-born**.
- The first blight appears.
- Choir cells recruit.

Responses:
- `stars`: warns courts.
- `sun`: scoffs.
- Burrowfolk: stockpile.

**Stage 2: The Waning.**

Trigger: an End portal is lit, or world day 120 or later.

Effects:
- Nights about 15% longer.
- Slower crops in cold biomes.
- Blight spreads by trade.
- Undead raid settlements with unremembered graves.
- More night-mares.
- Grain prices rise and refugees move.
- The Quiet Table appears.

Responses: realms argue over marching on the Hollow.
- Valemen kings smell a crown.
- The Burning Mirror preaches a crusade.
- Watchers and House Maervaine beg restraint.
- Dunmar Returners want star-iron.
- Red Ember clans want glory.

**Stage 3: The Breaking.**

Trigger: the Ender Dragon is killed.

Immediate event, **the Night of Falling Stars**:
- Three nights of meteors.
- Every settlement hears of it within a day.
- The Lanterns go dark and the Last Ember appears.

Ongoing effects:
- Stronger undead.
- Untended graves rise.
- Rotwood and hush-moss spread.
- Phantom swarms.
- Sun-faith fervour spikes, then collapses when no twin dawn comes.
- Choir membership surges.

The Last Ember becomes the most important object in the world:

| Faction | Wants to do with the Last Ember |
|---|---|
| Watchers and House Maervaine | Rekindle it at once. |
| Twin-Dawners | Break it under the noon sun. |
| Burning Mirror | Destroy it. |
| Dunmar Forge-Thanes | Forge an Unfading Fire from it, which kills it. |
| Valemen kings | Hold it. Whoever holds it claims the **High Crown**. |
| Ashborn | Fight over it. |
| Tidefolk | Sell it. |
| Skrell | Steal it. |
| Burrowfolk | Wish everyone would calm down and come inside. |
| The Choir | Carry it to the Lightless Door. |

**Stage 4: The Long Night.**

Trigger: 20 days after the Breaking with no Rekindling, or the Hush reaches 100 during stage 3.

Effects:
- A dim sun (darkness and fog tint) and short days.
- Crops grow only near light.
- **Night Tides**: mass undead assaults every few nights.
- The Choir may summon the Herald.
- Duskhounds roam.
- Famine wars.
- Realms fall or join a **Lantern League**.

Faith shifts: `sun` falls, `hearth` and `stars` rise, `veiled_choir` peaks.

**Stage 5: Resolution.** There are three possible outcomes.

1. **Rekindling.**
   - Trigger: the four Lanterns are placed and the Bound Star respawns.
   - The Night recedes over several days to a permanent **Afterdawn**, with stage 1 effects.
   - The leader earns the title **Rekindler** and great prestige.
   - The faction that rekindled pays a price. House Maervaine goes into the Dimming, the Dunmar lose their star-iron, or a player-allied settlement loses its oldest elder to the Lanterns, with a funeral event.
   - Slaying the new star restarts the Breaking, faster.
2. **The Unmaking.**
   - Trigger: the Choir brings the Last Ember to a Lightless Door during the Long Night.
   - The result is a permanent **Gloaming**: harsher nights, Choir realms, undead lords.
   - This is a configurable hard mode.
3. **The Twin Dawn.** Rumour only. If it is ever implemented, it must be ambiguous and costly: perhaps a sun that never sets, and a desert that grows.

### 10.3 What the player sees

Nobody ever says "the Dragon is Nahire". The player pieces it together from:
- The contradictory books `star_eater` and `what_burns_below`.
- Burrowfolk tales of the Lamp in the Cellar.
- Watch-Spire inscriptions.
- The price paid at the Rekindling.
- The plain fact that the Night only truly comes after they kill her.

## 11. Politics, Law and Power

### 11.1 Shared concepts

- **Realm.** A faction holding settlements under a ruler. Its name comes from `realm_patterns`.
- **Legitimacy (0 to 100)** comes from lineage, faith confirmation, victories, justice and full granaries. Low legitimacy invites rivals, coups and rebels.
- **Crimes.**
  - **Petty**: theft, brawling, poaching.
  - **Grave**: murder, arson, banditry, grave desecration.
  - **Unforgivable**: treason, Choir membership, unnaming, breaking guest-right.
- **Punishments.** From lightest to heaviest: fines, stocks, labour, flogging, branding, exile, death. Each culture favours some (11.2).
- **Trials** need an accuser, evidence and a culture-appropriate judge. Witnesses and found items count as evidence. Rumours raise suspicion but never convict. Bribed judges raise corruption.
- **Taxes** are collected in Reaping, plus culture duties. High taxes with low food breed unrest.
- **Corruption (0 to 100)** grows from bribes, nepotism and unpaid soldiers. It cuts tax yield and justice and feeds banditry.
- **Rebellion.** Sustained unrest becomes peasant risings, guild strikes, noble revolts or religious uprisings. Victorious rebels found new realms.

## 13. Connective Tissue

1. **Graves remember.** Each faith has a grave template (section 5). The grave entity stores `person`, `faith`, `death_day`, `last_tended`. An untended grave (candle unlit, grave-tree cut, name-stone defaced) becomes **unremembered** after a year. It raises the Hush and, from stage 2, may rise. Tending graves is a real NPC family task.
2. **The candle chain.** Burrowfolk make the candles for most funerals. If bandits or war cut the Honey Lane, candle prices rise, poor families cannot relight graves, and the dead go unremembered. A bandit camp in the Combes can cause a haunting in a Valemen town.
3. **Blight to the Choir.** Blight raises grain prices. Empty stores send households out as refugees. Settlements that refuse refugees lose legitimacy with the `hearth` and `grove` faithful. Unfed refugees find the Quiet Table, which seeds a hidden Choir cell. The cell robs graves, and the Hush rises.
4. **Faith shapes marriage** through the matrix in 5.10. Successful mixed marriages slowly warm opinion between cultures.
5. **Names carry the dead.** `ancestors` families prefer a dead grandparent's name. Eldrin choose names on Naming Nights. Ashborn adults may rename after a deed.
6. **Sun law shapes markets.** `sun` settlements stop trading at dusk. Foreign and skrell night-markets fill the gap and take the blame for crime.
7. **Graveyards grow forests.** Grove tree-burials become woods over generations. A lumber shortage can collide with a cemetery.
8. **Arrears become raids.** Ashborn companies that are paid late "collect" lawfully.
9. **Omen-born.** Children born in stages 1 to 4 get the `omen_born` trait. Watchers court them, Brands fear them, and the Choir recruits them.
10. **Eldrin decline makes ruins.** A house with no heir may go into the Dimming and abandon its settlement, leaving a lore-rich ruin.
11. **Death makes history.** Every dead settlement leaves a culture-styled ruin with its graves, books and a rumour.
12. **Unresolved deaths haunt.** Untried murders, or deaths without rites, may leave a grief-bound item. Justice clears it.
13. **Hearth-rolls are the census.** This is the in-world reason the genealogy UI and the hearth-tax work.
14. **Wyrm signs lead to hunts.** Precursor signs become `dragon_sighting` rumours. The realm then chooses tribute, a Wyrmhunt or evacuation. Slayers gain succession claims.
15. **The Night moves faith.** Each stage shifts conversion weights, so a playthrough visibly changes religious demographics.
16. **Sleep is law.** From stage 2, realms may pass curfews and sleep laws. Sleepless NPCs and players raise night-mare pressure and earn rumours about "the sleepless stranger".
